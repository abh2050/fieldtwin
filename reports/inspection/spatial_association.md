# Spatial association: proximity, not attribution

Generated by `pipelines/inspection/reports.py`. Rules: `config/inspection/gate6_association.yaml`, version 2.

## What was compared

455 Carbon Mapper methane plume origins against 645 registry gas wells whose coordinates Gate 2 accepts for spatial analysis (681 in the registry; QUARANTINED 1, UNRESOLVED_DATUM 35, USABLE 645).

A plume and a well are linked as `EMISSION_NEAR` when the plume origin lies within 250 m of the well. Every link is `UNVERIFIED`, carries no confidence value, and is DERIVED. The database refuses to mark one verified or to turn it into `SOURCE_ATTRIBUTED_TO`.

**Disclosures:** the distribution of plume-to-well distances and a uniform-box reference were looked at once, during source inspection, before the radius was fixed, so the other radii are reported beside it. The comparison method below (version 2) replaced the uniform-box baseline after technical review, and was chosen and run after the version 1 numbers were known.

## Proximity against a footprint-matched null

A plume can only be detected where Carbon Mapper observed. Scene footprints are far from uniform: the median scene with a plume covers 19% of the study area. So each plume is compared with 200 points drawn uniformly inside **the footprint of the scene that observed it**, clipped to the study area. That holds fixed where, when, and with which instrument the observation was made. 1 of 455 plume origins falls just outside its own quantized footprint and is kept.

Intervals resample the 142 scenes with plumes (2,000 resamples), not individual plumes, because plumes in one scene share conditions.

**Persistent sources.** The same source can be detected in many scenes. As a sensitivity check, plume origins that chain within 100 m of each other form one location group, and only the earliest plume of each group is kept (147 groups):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 m | 21.8% | 3.4% | +18.3 | +11.6 to +25.0 |
| 250 m | 41.5% | 16.8% | +24.7 | +15.7 to +33.3 |
| 500 m | 61.9% | 44.0% | +17.9 | +10.5 to +25.6 |
| 1000 m | 74.8% | 71.3% | +3.5 | -3.5 to +10.6 |

Within 250 m, plume origins lie near a registry gas well more often than matched points from the same scenes: 34.7% against 17.0%, and 41.5% against 16.8% with one plume per location group. At 1 km the intervals include zero.

**What this does and doesn't show.** Within the observed area, detected plumes sit closer to registry gas wells than an arbitrary observed location does. It doesn't show that any well is a source: wells sit on pads beside tanks, compressors, and pipelines that the registry omits, so a plume near a well is equally a plume near that equipment. The scene bootstrap is still optimistic, because location groups are a rough stand-in for sources and single linkage can merge neighbours.

## Why proximity can't name a source

- **Several candidates.** Of the 158 plumes with a well within 250 m, 45 have exactly one candidate, 72 have two to four, and 41 have five or more.
- **An incomplete registry.** The registry holds gas wells only. Oil wells, tank batteries, compressors, flares, and pipelines are absent, so the nearest registered well isn't necessarily the nearest infrastructure. One chip viewed during development shows a tank battery beside the registered pad; the chips have not otherwise been reviewed.
- **Uncertain locations.** Carbon Mapper states that plume representations may not precisely match infrastructure on the ground. OCD describes its well locations as approximate.
- **Wind and time.** A plume origin is an instantaneous estimate from one overpass.

Nearest-link distances among linked plumes, quartiles: 77, 122, 184 m. Links at other radii: 100 m: 141, 500 m: 1534, 1000 m: 4485.

## What this supports

A statement of the form "a plume origin was detected N metres from this registered well, and K other registered wells are also within the radius." Nothing in Gate 6 supports a leak at a well, an emission source, an equipment condition, or any link to AETHER Field.
