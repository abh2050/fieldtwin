# Inspection scheduling: a modeled comparison

> **Modeled scenario estimates for a fictional facility.** Every figure below comes from a simulation of fictional AETHER Field under declared assumptions (`config/decision/economics.yaml`, version 2). None is a realized saving, an expected saving, or a forecast for any real operation.

Generated by `pipelines/decision/reports.py` from `reports/decision/results/economics.json`.

## Setup

- **Horizon and replicates:** 365 days, 200 replicates, seed 20261110. Each replicate draws one year of faults and false alarms; all three strategies face the same draw, so their differences come from scheduling.
- **Assets:** 31 fictional assets at 16 inspection sites (12 well pads, 3 gathering segments, the compressor station), located on the Gate 4 network. Nominal flow through each asset is its median in the Gate 4 normal run.
- **Faults (assumed):** well 0.3, flowline 0.15, gathering_segment 0.2, compressor 0.6 per asset per year; 25% are sensor faults with no production loss. A physical fault defers 20% of the flow through its asset at $2.50 per Mcf until repaired.
- **Detector (measured in Gate 5, held-out regimes):** detection share DERATE 0.88, RESTRICTION 0.56, SENSOR 0.21; delays drawn between the Gate 5 quartiles; 8.0 false alarms a year. Wells and flowlines borrow the restriction results, an assumption, because Gate 5 has no well-specific category. 80% of real-fault alarms are assumed to target the faulted asset.
- **Field work (assumed):** one crew, 10 h a day, 2 h on site, 90% chance an inspection finds a physical fault, 1 day to repair, $120 an hour, $1.20 a km at 30 km/h with a 1.3 road factor.
- **Strategies:** fixed rounds every 30 days in a fixed geographic order; risk-based dispatch on each approved alarm (2 h approval delay), one out-and-back trip each, plus a round every 90 days; route-optimized, the same triggers with each day's alarms and round stops routed together by OR-Tools (261 distinct tours solved in the main run).

| Strategy | Total | Labor | Travel | Deferred production | Site visits | Alarm trips | Trips finding nothing | Days to repair |

| Fixed rounds | $285,201 ($111,939 to $591,115) | $51,949 | $609 | $232,643 | 208 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 18.8 |
| Risk-based | $386,676 ($88,030 to $988,091) | $23,256 | $336 | $363,084 | 92 | 12.6 | 9.3 | 30.2 |
| Route-optimized | $386,652 ($88,030 to $988,073) | $23,237 | $331 | $363,084 | 92 | 12.6 | 9.3 | 30.2 |

Paired differences, replicate by replicate:

| Comparison | Mean | 5th to 95th percentile | Replicates cheaper |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Risk-based minus fixed | $101,475 | −$151,393 to $516,185 | 32% |
| Route-optimized minus fixed | $101,451 | −$151,424 to $516,182 | 32% |
| Route-optimized minus risk-based | −$24 | −$86 to −$1 | 100% |

**Under these assumptions, fixed monthly rounds cost least on average.** Risk-based dispatch saves labor, but with the selected detector catching about half of restrictions, stretching rounds to 90 days leaves physical faults running longer, and deferred production dominates the cost. The spread is wide because a single fault on the trunk segment can dominate a year. **Route optimization changes almost nothing**: the field is about 4 km across and a day holds four or five stops, so there is little travel to save.

## Limitations

1. Every rate, cost, and probability is assumed for a fictional field. Only the detector's behaviour and the network's flows come from earlier gates, and both are themselves synthetic.
2. A dispatch that finds nothing produces no second alarm in this model, although a real detector would keep alarming on a persisting fault. That favours fixed rounds.
3. Detection delays are drawn between Gate 5 quartiles, and Gate 5's held-out regimes are themselves a small synthetic benchmark.
4. A strategy mixing alarm dispatch with monthly rounds wasn't declared before the run, so it isn't reported.
5. Crew capacity, travel, and repair are simplified: one crew, straight-line distances with a road factor, and repairs that always succeed once a fault is found.
